A full day of matches on a real field

On Saturday, October 10, System Overload Robotics hosted 10th Street Throwdown at 3100 E 10th Street in Oakland, the practice field of Highlander Robotics (8033). Eleven teams brought their REBUILT robots for a day of qualification matches, fuel everywhere, and practice for the drive teams ahead of fall competitions.

Scrimmages like this are where drive teams get reps, pit crews find what breaks, and new members see a match up close for the first time. We ran the whole event ourselves, from the schedule and refereeing to a field management system our team built for scoring the hubs.

How a REBUILT match is scored

Every fuel ball that lands in your alliance's hub while it's active is worth a point. The catch is that the hubs take turns: during the four teleop shifts, only one alliance can score at a time.

Transition10 s

Watchtower FMS

The hubs on the 10th Street field don't count balls, and an offseason event can't borrow FIRST's field system. So our team built its own.

Watchtower is a field management system that runs on one laptop over the venue WiFi. Cameras watch each hub opening, and a YOLO object-detection model counts every ball that goes in. Watchtower checks each count against the match clock to decide whether that alliance's hub was active, then adds climbs and fouls to produce the score.

  1. Scorekeeper starts the matchWatchtower builds the timeline of auto, transition, shifts, and endgame from that click.
  2. Hub cameras count fuelEach detected ball is logged with which hub it entered and when.
  3. Refs log fouls from their phonesMinor and major buttons for each alliance, with the fouling team tagged.
  4. The emcee calls the hub lightsTheir phone shows who won auto and counts down to every hub change.
  5. Scorekeeper reviews and commitsCounts can be corrected before a score is final. Committed results, rankings, and alliances are sent to The Blue Alliance automatically.
View Watchtower on GitHub Open watchtower.systemoverload.org

Screenshots use demo data.

Free and open source. Watchtower is MIT-licensed so any team can run its own REBUILT scrimmage. It also works without cameras: the scorekeeper can type fuel counts for each period instead.
